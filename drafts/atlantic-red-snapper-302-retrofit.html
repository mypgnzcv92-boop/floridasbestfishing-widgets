How We Got Here: The 39-Day Season That Never Opened

Earlier this year, Governor DeSantis and the FWC announced a 39-day Atlantic red snapper season for 2026 under an Exempted Fishing Permit (EFP) — May 22 through June 20 plus three October weekends, more than 1,800% over the two-day federal season anglers got in 2025. The plan handed Florida state management of the recreational Atlantic fishery in both state and federal waters. For a fishery that had been choked down to a long weekend for years, it was a huge deal.

Hours before opening day, a federal court in Washington, D.C. granted a preliminary injunction halting the EFPs for Florida, Georgia, South Carolina and North Carolina. NOAA Fisheries rescinded the permits, the FWC withdrew its executive order, and Atlantic state waters fell back to the standard rule in the table above. The summer window never opened.

The October Plan: What Florida Asked For

Florida, Georgia and South Carolina went back to NOAA with revised applications on July 10, rewritten to answer the court's concerns. NOAA published them for public comment August 10–25, and as of this update the agency is still reviewing them — no permits have been issued and no decision date has been announced.

Here's what Florida's version would look like if it's approved:

  • Dates: October 1–31, 2026, in Atlantic state and federal waters, with possible extra days in December if the harvest cap isn't reached. All harvest closed in November.
  • Bag limit: 1 red snapper per person per day, counted inside a 10-fish snapper-grouper aggregate on a declared red snapper trip.
  • Size limit: none proposed for Florida (South Carolina asked for a 20-inch minimum).
  • Harvest cap: 185,379 fish for Florida — when it's hit, it's over.
  • Gear: non-offset circle hooks and a descending device, rigged and ready.
  • Reporting: Florida's free State Reef Fish Survey — already mandatory if you bottom-fish from a private boat — plus dockside checks.
  • For-hire captains and crew couldn't keep a limit.

The thing to remember: none of this is in effect. Until NOAA issues the permits and the FWC confirms dates, October is a proposal, not a season. We'll update this page the day that changes.

Don't Confuse the Atlantic With the Gulf

The Gulf and Atlantic are managed completely separately, and the court ruling never touched the Gulf. The Gulf private-recreational season reopened September 1 — daily through October 4, then three-day weekends through Nov 1, two-day weekends through Nov 22, a Thanksgiving window, December weekends, Christmas and Jan 1–4. Gulf limits are 2 per person with a 16-inch minimum. If you're fishing out of the Panhandle, Big Bend or Southwest Florida, you're good to go — every date is in our Gulf red snapper season guide, and the rest of the Sept 1 openers are in the September report.

Where Atlantic Red Snapper Live

From Jacksonville and St. Augustine down through Port Canaveral, Sebastian and Fort Pierce, Atlantic red snapper stack on natural ledges, wrecks and artificial reefs in 70–150 feet — almost all of it past the three-mile line. That's why the state-waters opening is a footnote for most anglers and October is the real prize. If you'd rather let a captain find the ledge, book an offshore charter out of Northeast Florida and watch this page for the October call; the Southeast Coast guide and offshore hub cover the rest of the coast.

How to Be Ready for October

If NOAA says yes, the window is 31 days and the cap is finite — so be rigged before the first weekend. Red snapper hold on the same offshore structure as the rest of the reef crew: ledges, wrecks and artificial reefs in 60–120+ ft. A stout bottom outfit like a Penn Carnage III rod with a Penn Spinfisher VII 5500 (or step up to a Shimano Saragosa 8000 for the deeper stuff) handles them. Rig non-stainless, non-offset circle hooks on a knocker or Carolina rig with cut bait or live pinfish.

One non-negotiable, October or not: federal reef-fish rules require a descending device or venting tool rigged and ready on board, plus non-stainless circle hooks and a dehooking tool. With federal waters closed, every Atlantic red snapper you hook past three miles goes back — and a fish sent down on a descender survives; one floated on the surface doesn't.

Bottom Line

Florida fought for a much bigger Atlantic red snapper season, the courts hit pause, and the state has gone back with an October plan that's still sitting on NOAA's desk. Today you can keep two over 20 inches inside three miles and nothing past it. The Gulf is wide open through October 4.
