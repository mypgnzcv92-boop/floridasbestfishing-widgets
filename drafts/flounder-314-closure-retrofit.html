Flounder are the inshore fish hiding in plain sight. While everybody's chasing snook and reds, doormat flatfish are buried in the sand right under the dock you walked past, waiting to inhale anything that swims by. They don't chase, they don't school in plain view, and they fight like a wet towel — but a fat flounder is one of the best eating fish that swims in Florida, and catching them is a quiet art most anglers never learn. Here's how to put a limit in the box.

One date to circle: October 15. Flounder harvest closes statewide that morning and stays shut through November 30. The weeks before it are when the fall push toward the inlets begins — so if you want flounder this year, the window is now. Jump to the fall-run plan ↓

Meet the flounder: Florida's ambush predator

Florida has two main players. The gulf flounder is the only one found along the entire Florida coast; it favours sand, and you can tell it by three dark, eye-like spots set in a triangle. The southern flounder is the bigger fish — FWC puts it at "common to 20 inches" against about 14 for the gulf — but it is generally found only in the northern half of the state: north of the Loxahatchee River on the Atlantic and north of the Caloosahatchee on the Gulf. It prefers mud and silt, and its spots aren't eye-like. (Sources: FWC species profiles and FWRI's flounder status report.)

Both are flat, bottom-camouflaged ambush hunters that lie buried on the bottom with just their eyes showing, then rocket up to engulf bait that passes overhead. Understand that one behavior and you understand how to catch them: you have to put your bait on the bottom, near structure, and crawl it slowly.

Where to find flounder

Flounder relate to two things: structure and a current edge that funnels bait. Hit these:

  • Dock and bridge pilings — they sit on the down-current side and ambush bait swept past.
  • Inlet and pass edges — sandy drop-offs near moving water are flounder highways, especially in fall.
  • Channel edges and grass-flat drop-offs — the seam where sand meets grass.
  • Oyster bars and creek mouths — anywhere bait gets pinched into a chokepoint.
  • Jetties and rock piles — the sand pockets between rocks hold fish.

The common thread is a hard or transitional bottom next to moving water. Flounder face into the current and wait, so work the up-current side and let your bait wash to them.

The fall run: you have until October 14

Here's why the closure lands where it does. Both Florida species leave the bays to spawn offshore in late fall and winter, in roughly 65 to 200 feet of water (FWRI). FWC closes harvest from October 15 because that is when the pre-spawning aggregations form — the whole point is to let those fish reach the spawn.

The logic for you as an angler follows from that. The route from the backwater to deep water runs through the passes and inlets, so from late September into mid-October the inlet and pass edges in the list above matter more every week. The open window is the early part of the migration; the heaviest bunching-up happens after the gate shuts.

  • Move toward the mouth. Trade the back-bay docks for the sandy drop-offs, jetty sand pockets and bridge pilings nearest the inlet. Our jetty fishing guide covers the rock, and the pier fishing guide covers the structures that span the passes.
  • Nothing else about the method changes. Moving water, bait on the bottom, slow crawl, three-count before the hookset.
  • Gigging counts too. Gigs and spears are legal flounder gear, but the closure is a closure on harvest, whatever you use.
  • After the 14th, every flounder goes back, including the one that grabs a redfish bait. Release it quickly and keep it in the water. The season checker near the top of this page flips to closed on its own on October 15.
📅 Plan around it: Open now → closed October 15 – November 30 → reopens December 1. While flounder are off the table, the bull redfish fall run and seatrout share the same passes and flats.

Soft-plastic swimming mullet / paddletailSlow, steady drag with bottom contactScented baits like Gulp shine for flounder

The golden rule: slow it down and keep it on the bottom. If you're not occasionally ticking sand and structure, your bait is too high and a flounder won't leave the bottom to chase it. A bucktail jig tipped with a Gulp swimming mullet is the deadliest searching combo there is.

The flounder bite — and how to not blow the hookset

A flounder bite feels like weight, then a thump — often you'll think you snagged the bottom. They grab the bait sideways and reposition it before swallowing. The classic move: when you feel that "stuck" feeling, drop your rod tip, give it a slow three-count, then reel tight and lift. Set too soon and you'll pull the bait right out of its mouth. Patience puts flatfish in the boat.

Flounder rod, reel and rig

You don't need anything fancy — a 7' medium spinning rod with a 2500–3000 reel, 10–15 lb braid and a 20 lb fluorocarbon leader covers it. A simple Penn Battle spinning combo is a perfect, durable flounder setup. Most anglers fish a 1/4–3/8 oz jighead or a fishfinder (Carolina) rig so the bait stays pinned to the bottom where flounder live.

🎣 Best paired with: A sensitive braided line is huge for flounder — that subtle "thump" is easy to miss on mono. Run braid to a fluorocarbon leader and you'll feel every tick of the bottom.

Florida flounder regulations (2026)

Verified against FWC's flounder page on September 16, 2026. Regulations change — check before you keep a fish.

  • Minimum size: 14 inches total length
  • Bag limit: 5 per person, per day
  • Closed season: October 15 – November 30, in all Florida state and federal waters; reopens December 1
  • Legal gear: hook and line, gigs, spears, cast net and seine. Multiple hooks used with natural bait are prohibited, and so is snatching

The rules are identical on both coasts — no Gulf/Atlantic split — and cover gulf, southern, summer and fringed flounder.

Bottom line: Flounder reward the angler who slows down. Find structure next to moving water, crawl a jig or live mullet right on the bottom, give the bite a three-count before you set, and you'll start filling the box with the best-eating fish in the bay. They're hiding closer than you think — just get it done before October 15, because after that every one goes back until December.
