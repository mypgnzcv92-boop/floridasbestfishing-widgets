Season Update · Atlantic Red Snapper

It's official: Florida's 2026 Atlantic red snapper season is October 9–22 — fourteen straight days in state and federal waters, one fish per person, no minimum size. After a court killed the summer season, this is the whole year in a two-week window, and you cannot keep a fish unless you declare the trip first.

What Changed on September 22

NOAA Fisheries issued Florida a revised Exempted Fishing Permit (EFP 26-SERO-07) on September 21, 2026. The FWC signed Executive Order 26-33 the next day, setting the season. Here is the whole rule set in one table.

The Dates You'll See Elsewhere Are Wrong

This matters more than the season itself, because the old dates are still sitting on half the fishing sites on the internet.

Back in May, Florida announced the Atlantic fall as three separate weekends — October 2–4, 9–11 and 16–18. That plan died with the injunction. The revised permit replaced it with one continuous block, October 9 through 22.

If you fish the old calendar you get it wrong twice: October 2–4 is closed — harvesting then is illegal — and you'd sit out October 12–15 and 19–22, which are open. Eight legal days most people are about to miss.

One more distinction worth knowing: the federal South Atlantic season, the one that applies to states without an EFP, is October 9–11 only. Florida's longer window exists because of the state permit — which is exactly why you have to be able to show it.

Declare the Trip, or You Can't Keep One

This is the part that will cost people fish. The season is open access — no lottery, no draw — but it runs on a trip declaration.

  • Anglers 16 and older must declare each trip in the web-based FWC Atlantic Red Snapper Reporting System before leaving the dock. FWC's wording is blunt: anglers who do not declare a red snapper trip may not keep red snapper.
  • Carry a copy of the EFP on board for every red snapper trip. Download it from FWC's season page and keep it with your licence.
  • Private anglers need a saltwater fishing licence (unless exempt) plus the free State Reef Fish Angler designation.
  • For-hire operations must register for the free Atlantic For-Hire Reef Fish Registry to take part. If you are the paying customer on a for-hire trip, you do not declare — the operation handles it.
  • FWC also asks for post-trip reporting and dockside survey cooperation. That data is what decides whether a season like this happens again.

The 10-Fish Aggregate Changes How You Bottom Fish

On a declared red snapper trip you are working inside a 10-fish snapper-grouper aggregate — a single bucket for the whole reef crew, not a stack of separate limits. Once you hit ten, you stop bottom fishing for reef fish. You can keep fishing for dolphin, kings, cobia or anything else that isn't on the list.

  • Only 1 may be a red snapper
  • Only 1 may be a black or scamp grouper (gag is out of season)
  • Up to 2 red, yellowfin, yellowmouth, coney, graysby, red hind or rock hind grouper
  • Only 1 greater amberjack; only 1 red porgy or tilefish (tilefish out of season)
  • Up to 2 black sea bass; up to 5 gray triggerfish
  • Up to 7 schoolmaster, gray, lane, yellowtail, queen, silk or blackfin snapper; up to 5 vermilion or mutton

Everything except red snapper still follows its own season, size and vessel limits. The aggregate is a ceiling, not a permission slip.

What About Right Now, Before October 9?

Treat Atlantic red snapper as closed until opening morning. Federal waters have been shut all year, and the FWC closed Atlantic state waters to red snapper harvest on September 22 to line the closure up with the October season.

Two honest caveats, because this is exactly the kind of detail that gets anglers written up. FWC's general snapper regulations page still displays the standing Atlantic state-waters entry — 20-inch minimum, two per person — because that is the default rule the executive order suspends. The executive order is what governs. If you are on the water before October 9, the safe and legal call is to release every red snapper, and to check FWC's Atlantic red snapper page the morning of your trip.

Federal reef-fish rules apply the whole time: a descending device or venting tool rigged and ready, non-stainless non-offset circle hooks, and a dehooking tool. A fish sent back down on a descender lives. One left floating on the surface does not, and with a two-week season and a harvest cap, every dead discard is a fish nobody gets to keep.

How We Got Here

Earlier this year Florida announced a 39-day Atlantic red snapper season under an Exempted Fishing Permit — May 22 to June 20 plus those October weekends, more than 1,800% of the two-day federal season anglers got in 2025. Hours before opening day, a federal court in Washington, D.C. granted a preliminary injunction halting the permits for Florida, Georgia, South Carolina and North Carolina. NOAA rescinded them, FWC withdrew its order, and the summer window never opened.

Florida, Georgia and South Carolina went back to NOAA in July with revised applications written to answer the court. NOAA took public comment in August and issued the three permits on September 21 — with harvest caps of 83,720 fish for Florida, 4,859 for Georgia and 8,695 for South Carolina. North Carolina did not reapply.

FWC has said it will review October's landings and effort data with NOAA to decide whether a second season happens in December. That decision rides on how this fortnight goes — which is the real reason to declare your trips and answer the surveys.

Don't Confuse the Atlantic With the Gulf

They are managed separately, and the court case never touched the Gulf. The Gulf private-recreational season has been open daily since September 1 through October 4, is then open daily October 9–22 as well, and continues on weekends and holiday windows into January 4, 2027. Gulf limits are two per person at a 16-inch minimum — a different size limit, a different bag, a different coast. Every Gulf date is in our Gulf red snapper season guide.

Where They Live, and Being Ready for the 9th

From Jacksonville and St. Augustine down through Port Canaveral, Sebastian and Fort Pierce, Atlantic red snapper stack on natural ledges, wrecks and artificial reefs in 70–150 feet — almost all of it past the three-mile line, which is why a season that includes federal water is the whole story.

Fourteen days is not long enough to shake down tackle. A stout bottom outfit — a Penn Carnage III rod with a Penn Spinfisher VII 5500, or a Shimano Saragosa 8000 for the deeper drops — on a knocker or Carolina rig with non-stainless circle hooks and cut bait or live pinfish covers it. Bottom Line

Florida's Atlantic red snapper season is October 9 through 22, state and federal waters, one fish per person, no minimum size, inside a 10-fish snapper-grouper aggregate. Declare every trip before you leave the dock and carry the EFP, or you cannot keep a fish. Ignore the old October weekend dates still floating around — they are not in effect. Before the 9th, release everything. And if the data comes back clean, there may be a December encore.
